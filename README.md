## Research

BEng Computer Science, **First Class Honours** — Anglia Ruskin University, Cambridge (2026).

My dissertation was an empirical study of whether RAG grounding improves rubric alignment and trustworthiness in AI-generated feedback — evaluated against expert annotation across 100 reports. The central finding is not just that RAG improves alignment scores, but that it makes AI errors **inspectable and contestable** rather than hidden: every feedback claim was traceable to a retrieved rubric passage, enabling human reviewers to interrogate outputs rather than accept them.

This connects to a broader question I care about: what does it take for an AI system to be **trustworthy rather than merely fluent**?

## Why These Projects Matter

**EduAIPlatform** is an empirical study in making AI outputs trustworthy rather than merely fluent:
- RAG grounding makes every claim **traceable** — errors are inspectable, not hidden
- The confidence gate implements **human oversight as an enforceable technical control**
- LangGraph fallback routing maintains **audit-complete output under degraded conditions**
- Local inference enforces **data minimisation by architecture**, not by promise

**NebulaKV + InferX** demonstrate systems depth relevant to ML infrastructure: distributed consensus, failure handling, P99 latency benchmarking, C++20/23 — the same skills required for high-performance ML systems work.
